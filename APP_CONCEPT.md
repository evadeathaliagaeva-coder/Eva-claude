# Eva — Women's Sexual Health & Wellness App
## Full UX/UI Product Concept

---

## 1. Product Concept

### App Name
**Eva** — a personal name, not a word. Intentionally human. Not symbolic, not spiritual, not clinical.

### Tagline
*"Your body knows things. This is how you start listening."*

### Positioning
Eva sits between a smart health journal and a quiet personal coach. It is not a medical device, a sex-positive influencer, or a period tracker with pink flowers. It is a calm, private space where a woman can notice what is happening in her body and desire — without being told what to do about it.

Eva belongs in the same lifestyle category as a good sleep tracker, a therapy app, or a mindful eating journal — except it deals with the specific territory most women's health apps either avoid entirely or overcomplicate.

### Core Value Proposition
Eva helps women:
- Track their libido, arousal, mood, pain, cycle, and sexual experience over time.
- Recognize patterns they couldn't see before — "my desire drops the week before my period" or "sex feels better when I'm less anxious."
- Know that what they're experiencing is normal, or understand when something is worth exploring professionally.
- Feel less alone and less broken.

### Emotional Promise
*"You will not leave this app feeling judged, rushed, or overwhelmed. You will leave it knowing a little more about yourself."*

### What Eva Is
- A private daily diary for sexual health and desire.
- A pattern-recognition tool that connects libido, mood, cycle, and symptoms.
- A gentle guide toward professional help when needed.
- An emotionally intelligent space that asks questions carefully.
- A lifestyle wellness product you don't have to hide.

### What Eva Is Not
- A diagnostic or medical tool.
- A sex-positive influencer content feed.
- A fertility app rebranded.
- A dating or intimacy app.
- A therapy replacement.
- A product that promises to "fix" your libido or sexual experience.
- A spiritual or "divine feminine" brand.

## 2. Product Principles

**1. One question at a time.**
Never stack sensitive questions. Give each one space. Move on without pressure.

**2. Skip is always available.**
No check-in field is mandatory. The user logs what she wants to log.

**3. Patterns, not conclusions.**
Eva shows what it notices. It never tells the user what it means. Language is always probabilistic: "may be connected," "you might notice," "worth exploring."

**4. Privacy is not a feature — it is the foundation.**
The product earns trust through behavior: PIN/biometric lock, neutral push notifications, transparent data explanations, no external sharing by default.

**5. Nothing is wrong with you.**
The default emotional register is normalization. Low libido is not failure. Pain is information. Discomfort is worth listening to.

**6. Gentle escalation, not alarm.**
When the app notices patterns that may warrant professional attention, it says so clearly but calmly. It never panics the user. It always tells her she is the one who decides what to do.

**7. No streak guilt.**
The app tracks consistency gently. Missing days is not punished. Re-entry is frictionless.

**8. The user owns her data.**
She can export, delete, or limit what is tracked at any time. No dark patterns around data retention.

### Habit Loop
**Trigger:** Neutral push notification ("Time to check in") or app icon routine.
**Action:** 2–4 minute daily check-in.
**Variable Reward:** New insight generated, or a short piece of content matched to today's state.
**Investment:** Over time, pattern insights become more specific and valuable — creating a compounding reason to return.

## 4. Onboarding Structure

**Design principle:** Onboarding feels like a first conversation, not a form. One screen per question. A progress indicator is visible but quiet. Every question has a "Skip" or "Prefer not to say" option. Sensitive questions are introduced with a one-sentence explanation of why Eva is asking.

Total: **14 screens** (including welcome and summary).

## 5. Screen-by-Screen UX

---

### Screen: Onboarding

**Purpose:** Establish trust, personalize the experience, collect just enough context to make the product useful from day one.

**Main user goal:** Feel safe enough to be honest. Feel seen without feeling interrogated.

**Layout:**
- Single question per screen, centered.
- Progress bar: thin, muted, top of screen. Percentage not shown — just a fill line.
- Question as large, calm headline.
- Sub-explanation in smaller, softer weight.
- Answer options as full-width soft pill cards or radio chips.
- Skip always in the top right or below CTA.
- Back arrow always available.

**Content hierarchy:**
1. Question (large, calm, top third)
2. Sub-explanation (small, below question)
3. Answer options (main interaction zone)
4. Primary CTA
5. Skip/Back

**Example copy:**
- "What's brought you here?" (not "What are your goals?")
- "Roughly — approximate is fine." (not "Enter your cycle length")
- "There's no right answer here." (before sensitive questions)

**CTA:** "Continue" (not "Next" — more conversational)

**Sensitive states:**
- If user selects "There's some history that makes this complicated" (Step 9): gentle acknowledgment appears inline, not as an alert.
- If user skips most questions: onboarding completes normally, with a note that they can update preferences anytime in settings.
- If the user abandons onboarding mid-way and returns: resumes from last question, not from beginning.

**Privacy note:** Steps 2 and 13 explicitly address privacy. No sensitive data from onboarding is shared externally.

---

**Purpose:** Give the user a calm, familiar daily starting point that doesn't demand anything of her.

**Main user goal:** See where she is today. Feel a gentle pull to check in.

**Layout:**
```
STATUS BAR
─────────────────────────────────
  [Eva wordmark]         [Profile icon]

  Good morning, Sofia.         ← personalized
  It's Wednesday.

─────────────────────────────────
  TODAY'S CHECK-IN CARD
  "How are you feeling today?"
  [Start check-in →]
─────────────────────────────────

  CYCLE / CONTEXT CARD (if enabled)
  "Day 18 of your cycle"
  Luteal phase · Energy often shifts this week

─────────────────────────────────
  INSIGHT PREVIEW CARD
  "You've checked in 5 days in a row."
  "A pattern is starting to show."
  [See your insights →]

─────────────────────────────────
  RECOMMENDED FOR YOU
  [Card: Educational content or exercise]
  Matched to today's state / cycle phase

─────────────────────────────────
  AI ASSISTANT ENTRY
  "Have a question? Eva is here."
  [Ask Eva →]

─────────────────────────────────
  BOTTOM NAVIGATION
  Home | Check-in | Insights | Explore | You
─────────────────────────────────
```

**Content hierarchy:**
1. Greeting + date (context, warmth)
2. Today's check-in CTA (primary action)
3. Cycle card (contextual, optional)
4. Insight preview (retention hook)
5. Recommendation (value, exploration)
6. AI assistant (deeper support)

**Example copy:**
- Greeting: "Good morning, Sofia." (name if provided, otherwise just "Good morning.")
- Cycle card: "Day 18. Luteal phase. Energy and desire often shift this week — that's completely normal."
- Insight preview: "Five check-ins in. A pattern is beginning to form."
- After check-in completed: "Today's check-in is done. You're building something useful."

**CTA:** "Start today's check-in" (primary, visible, soft)

**Empty states:**
- No check-ins yet: "Your first check-in will take about two minutes. It's the beginning of a real picture."
- Cycle tracking off: Cycle card is hidden. No mention.
- New user (day 1): Check-in card leads, no insight card shown yet.

**Sensitive states:**
- If user logged pain consistently: a soft card may appear: "We've noticed you've mentioned discomfort a few times. There's more on this in Explore when you're ready."

**Privacy note:** Home screen visible behind app lock. No check-in content visible without unlock.

### Screen: Daily Check-in

**Purpose:** Let the user log her state quickly, privately, and without judgment. Every field is optional.

**Main user goal:** Log today's experience in under 3 minutes.

**Layout:**
```
BACK ARROW     [Today's check-in]     [Save — top right, inactive until any log]

─────────────────────────────────
  SECTION 1: MOOD
  "How are you feeling right now?"
  [Horizontal icon-based selector]
  Rough | Low | Neutral | Okay | Good | Light
  (6 options, minimal labels, soft emoji-adjacent icons)

─────────────────────────────────
  SECTION 2: DESIRE
  "How present is desire today?"
  [Slider: 0 to 5, custom labels]
  Not at all ←————————————→ Strong

  "Arousal — separate from desire:"
  [Same slider style]
  None ←————————————→ Heightened

─────────────────────────────────
  SECTION 3: BODY COMFORT
  "Any pain or discomfort today?"
  [Slider: 0 to 5]
  None ←————————————→ Significant

  If > 2: soft prompt appears:
  "You can add a note about where or when — or skip it."

─────────────────────────────────
  SECTION 4: SYMPTOMS & CYCLE (if cycle tracking on)
  "Any of these today?" (multi-select tags)
  [Spotting] [Cramps] [Tender breasts]
  [Fatigue] [Bloating] [Headache] [PMS]
  [None of these]

─────────────────────────────────
  SECTION 5: SEXUAL ACTIVITY (optional, collapsed by default)
  "Add activity context?" [small toggle to expand]

  If expanded:
  Did you masturbate today? [Yes / No / Skip]
  Did you have sex with a partner? [Yes / No / Skip]
  If yes: Was it satisfying? [Slider: Not at all → Very much]
  Did you experience orgasm? [Yes / No / Not sure / Skip]

  Discomfort during sex? [Yes / No / Skip]
  If yes: [mild] [moderate] [significant]

─────────────────────────────────
  SECTION 6: OPTIONAL NOTE
  "Anything else worth noting?"
  [Soft multiline input, placeholder: "A few words is enough."]
  Character limit: 300

─────────────────────────────────
  REASSURANCE LINE (above CTA)
  "Nothing here is permanent. You can edit or delete any check-in."

─────────────────────────────────
  [Save check-in]  ← primary CTA, full width
─────────────────────────────────
```

**Content hierarchy:**
1. Mood (easiest entry, no friction)
2. Desire/arousal (core tracking)
3. Body comfort/pain (important but optional)
4. Symptoms (cycle-connected)
5. Activity (collapsed by default, most sensitive)
6. Note (free text, always optional)

**Example copy:**
- Section intros: "How are you feeling right now?" / "How present is desire today?" / "Any pain or discomfort today?"
- Slider labels: "Not at all" to "Strong" — no clinical numbers shown.
- Activity section label: "Add activity context?" not "Log sexual activity."
- Bottom reassurance: "Nothing here is permanent. You can edit or delete any check-in."

**CTA:** "Save check-in" — active as soon as any field is filled. Tapping "Save" with nothing filled asks: "Nothing logged today — that's fine. Mark the day anyway?" [Yes, mark it] [Actually, skip today]

**Sensitive states:**
- Pain slider > 3: small inline note: "Pain at this level is worth paying attention to. If it's recurring, Eva will let you know."
- If recurring severe pain pattern detected (3+ days): insight card surfaces on home screen. Never during check-in.
- Activity section collapsed by default every time — not remembered open, for privacy.

**Privacy note:** Activity section is collapsed and never shown in notification previews, widgets, or app previews.

### Screen: Insight

**Purpose:** Show patterns without overclaiming. Make the user feel that tracking is worthwhile.

**Main user goal:** Understand what her data is showing — without being told what it means.

**Layout:**
```
BACK / HEADER   [Insights]   [Filter: Week | Month]

─────────────────────────────────
  SUMMARY HEADER
  "The past 7 days."

  [Mini calendar strip — 7 dots, colored by mood/libido logged]
  [Days with data filled, days without neutral]

─────────────────────────────────
  PATTERN CARD (primary, prominent)
  Title: "Desire has been lower this week."
  Body: "This may be connected to where you are in your cycle —
  you're in the late luteal phase, when energy and desire often
  naturally dip. This isn't unusual."

  [Subtle graph: libido line + cycle phase overlay]

─────────────────────────────────
  MOOD × DESIRE CONNECTION (if data supports)
  "On days you logged feeling low or rough,
  desire also tended to be lower."
  [Small scatter / bar visual]

─────────────────────────────────
  PAIN / DISCOMFORT LOG (if relevant)
  "You mentioned discomfort 2 times this week."
  [See logged entries →]
  If pattern: "This has appeared in 3 of the last 4 weeks.
  It may be worth exploring with a professional."

─────────────────────────────────
  RECOMMENDATION CARD
  "For where you are right now:"
  [Card: Article / exercise / reflection prompt]
  Example: "A short exercise on body awareness — for moments
  when desire feels distant."
  [Read → or Start →]

─────────────────────────────────
  PROFESSIONAL SUPPORT CARD (conditional)
  Only shown when patterns warrant it.
  "When is it worth talking to someone?"
  "Recurring pain during sex, persistent loss of desire
  combined with distress, or unusual symptoms can all
  benefit from professional support. Eva can't tell you
  what's wrong — but it can tell you when something
  is worth exploring."
  [Find a specialist →] (informational link, not a referral)

─────────────────────────────────
  NEXT ACTION
  "What would you like to do next?"
  [Log today's check-in] | [Explore content]

─────────────────────────────────
```

**Content hierarchy:**
1. Summary strip (orientation)
2. Primary pattern insight (most valuable)
3. Mood/desire connection (supporting)
4. Pain log (safety, escalation)
5. Recommendation (value delivery)
6. Professional support (when needed, never alarming)
7. Next action

**Example copy:**
- "Desire has been lower this week. This may be connected to where you are in your cycle."
- "You've logged discomfort twice this week. If this is recurring, it's worth keeping track of."
- "Nothing stands out this week. That's useful to know too."
- Professional support: "Eva can't tell you what's wrong — but it can tell you when something is worth exploring."

**CTA:** "Log today's check-in" (if not done) | "Explore what might help" (if done)

**Empty states:**
- 0–2 check-ins: "Keep going — patterns take a little time to form. After 5–7 check-ins, Eva will have something real to show you."
- No pain logged: pain card not shown.
- No cycle data: cycle correlation card not shown.

**Sensitive states:**
- Recurring severe pain (3+ weeks): professional support card appears. Language is calm, never alarming.
- User logs distress note + very low libido for 10+ consecutive days: gentle message: "You've been tracking some difficult days. If you've been feeling this way persistently, talking to someone — a therapist, a doctor — might be a genuinely useful step."
- Trauma-adjacent language in notes: no AI analysis. Simple: "We noticed you've been reflecting a lot. Take your time."

## 7. UI Copy System

### Voice principles
- Warm without being syrupy.
- Direct without being clinical.
- Curious, not prescriptive.
- Uses contractions naturally.
- Never uses: "amazing," "journey," "goddess," "sacred," "healing," "manifest."
- Uses: "notice," "explore," "worth," "naturally," "often," "can be."

---

### Microcopy patterns

**Empty state — no check-in yet:**
> "Your first check-in will take about two minutes. It's the beginning of a real picture."

**Empty state — not enough data for insights:**
> "Patterns take a little time to form. After a few more check-ins, Eva will have something real to show you."

**Post check-in confirmation:**
> "Done. You're building something useful."

**Skipping a question:**
> "That's okay — you can always come back to this."

**Low libido normalization:**
> "Low desire is not a failure. It's information."

**Pain acknowledgment:**
> "Pain is worth listening to. You don't have to push through it."

**Returning after missing days:**
> "Welcome back. Nothing to catch up on — just today."

**AI assistant welcome:**
> "Ask me anything. I'm here to help you understand, not to judge."

**Professional support prompt (calm):**
> "Eva can't tell you what's wrong — but it can tell you when something might be worth a conversation with a professional."

**Streak — gentle, no guilt:**
> "Six days in a row. You're getting to know yourself."

**Streak broken:**
> "One missed day changes nothing about the pattern you've built."

**Privacy confirmation:**
> "This stays between you and Eva."

**Notification text:**
> "Eva · Time to check in" (no personal data, no mention of what was tracked)

**Partner communication prompt:**
> "Sometimes the hardest part is finding the words. Eva can help with that."

**After logging significant pain:**
> "Thanks for logging this. If it continues, it's worth keeping track of — and worth mentioning to a doctor or pelvic health specialist."

---

- "How are you feeling right now?" (not "Mood log")
- "How present is desire today?" (not "Rate your libido")
- "Any pain or discomfort today?" (not "Pain level")
- "Anything else worth noting?" (not "Add notes")

## 8. Visual Direction

### Art direction statement
Eva looks like a thoughtful editorial lifestyle brand. Clean pages, considered space, real photography. Nothing is trying too hard. The visual language communicates: "This was made by adults, for adults."

Think: the art direction of a high-quality sleep or mindfulness brand, but warmer. Reference the print aesthetic of a Kinfolk-era lifestyle magazine, with the clarity of a modern health app.

---

### Color palette

| Token | Value | Usage |
|-------|-------|-------|
| `--color-canvas` | #FAF5F0 | Main background. Warm off-white. |
| `--color-surface` | #F4EDE5 | Card backgrounds. Slightly deeper. |
| `--color-surface-mid` | #EAE0D6 | Dividers, secondary surfaces. |
| `--color-peach` | #E8C4A8 | Primary accent. Warm, skin-toned. |
| `--color-peach-deep` | #C9956F | CTA buttons, active states. |
| `--color-fig` | #4A3728 | Primary text. Deep warm brown. |
| `--color-walnut` | #7A5C48 | Secondary text, labels. |
| `--color-ash` | #B0A097 | Tertiary text, placeholders, disabled. |
| `--color-slate-warm` | #8D8078 | Chart lines, subtle UI elements. |
| `--color-alert-soft` | #F2D9C4 | Gentle warning states (not red). |
| `--color-alert-text` | #7A3E1A | Alert text (warm, not alarming). |
| `--color-white` | #FFFFFF | Pure white. Used sparingly. |

**What is never used:** hot pink, neon coral, medical blue, clinical white, purple, or black with high contrast.

---

### Typography direction

**Typeface stack:**
- **Display / Headlines:** A humanist serif — something like *Freight Text*, *Canela*, or *Tiempos Text*. Elegant, readable, a little literary. Used for greetings, insight headlines, onboarding questions.
- **Body / UI:** A clean geometric sans-serif — *DM Sans*, *Plus Jakarta Sans*, or *Inter* with slightly wider tracking. Neutral but warm. Used for labels, buttons, body text.
- **Accent / Data labels:** Monospace or tabular numerals from the body font stack. Used for cycle day counts, sliders, chart labels.

**Type scale:**
| Token | Size | Weight | Usage |
|-------|------|--------|-------|
| `--text-display` | 28px | 400 (serif) | Major headlines |
| `--text-title` | 22px | 500 (sans) | Screen titles |
| `--text-heading` | 18px | 500 (sans) | Section headings |
| `--text-body` | 16px | 400 (sans) | Body copy |
| `--text-small` | 14px | 400 (sans) | Labels, captions |
| `--text-micro` | 12px | 400 (sans) | Legal, timestamps |

---

### Shape language
- Radius tokens: 8px (inputs), 16px (cards), 24px (sheets, modals), 32px (pill buttons).
- No sharp corners anywhere in the UI.
- Cards have soft shadow: 0 2px 12px rgba(74, 55, 40, 0.08).
- Sliders: custom rounded track, filled with peach gradient.
- No hard borders on cards — use shadow or background color differentiation.

---

### Photography direction

**What to use:**
- Morning light on skin, fabric, hair — soft and slightly golden.
- Hands: holding a coffee cup, resting on bed sheets, touching the nape of a neck.
- Urban apartment environments: bathroom counter, unmade bed, beside table, kitchen counter.
- Women alone, in real moments: looking out a window, sitting on a floor, in a towel after a shower.
- Detail shots: texture of fabric, steam from a mug, light through a curtain.

**Treatment:** Slightly desaturated, warm-toned. Not over-filtered. Feels like a film photograph. Slight grain is acceptable.

**What to avoid:**
- Smiling women in studio settings.
- Any explicit anatomy or nudity.
- Posed gestures (hand on hip, perfect posture).
- Floral, petal, moon, water imagery as metaphors for sexuality.
- Any image that reads as "stock photo."
- Couples in bed with flawless hair.

---

### Icon style
- Line icons, 1.5px stroke, warm fig color.
- Slightly rounded caps.
- No filled icons except for active state in navigation.
- Custom icon set — not Material Icons or SF Symbols defaults.
- Size: 20px in navigation, 24px in UI, 32px in feature cards.

---

### Illustration and image usage
- Minimal illustration — Eva is primarily photographic.
- If illustrations are used: loose, editorial ink-style line drawings. Body-positive but not literal body outline illustrations.
- Data visualizations: muted, warm line charts. No bright colors. No pie charts.
- Absolutely no uterus, vulva, or anatomical illustrations in the UI surface.

## 10. Prototype Behavior

### After onboarding
- User lands on Home screen for the first time.
- Check-in card is prominent, with copy: "Your first check-in. It takes about two minutes."
- Cycle card appears only if cycle tracking was enabled in onboarding.
- Insight section shows: "Patterns take a few check-ins to form. Come back tomorrow."
- Recommendation card surfaces one piece of content matched to onboarding goals.

### Daily check-in behavior
- User opens check-in from home or center nav button.
- All sliders start at a neutral midpoint — not zero (avoids biasing toward neutral).
- Sections are visible but not pre-expanded — user scrolls through.
- Activity context section is always collapsed by default.
- "Save" button becomes active when any field is touched.
- After saving: brief success animation (subtle, not celebratory). Return to home with updated check-in card state: "Done for today."
- If user opens check-in again same day: existing data pre-filled, header says "Edit today's check-in."

### Insight generation behavior
- Insights available after 5 cumulative check-ins.
- Updated every day after a new check-in is saved.
- Insights are generated by:
  - Rolling 7-day window for patterns.
  - Cycle phase overlay (if enabled).
  - Mood × libido correlation.
  - Pain frequency tracking (separate logic, safety-flagged at 3+ occurrences in 3 weeks).
- Language is always hedged ("may be connected," "tends to be," "often").
- No insight ever says "you have X" or "this means Y."

### Return loop — day 2+
- Notification: "Eva · Time to check in" (neutral, never reveals logged content).
- Home screen greeting changes daily with soft variations.
- If 7+ check-ins logged: home screen insight preview updates.
- If 14+ check-ins: cycle correlation visible (if cycle enabled).
- After missed days: no guilt messaging. Re-entry screen: "Welcome back. Nothing to catch up on — just today."

### Premium content hint behavior
- Premium content cards appear in Explore and occasionally in Recommendation slots.
- They are not blurred or locked aggressively — they show a title and one-line description.
- A soft "Premium" label pill appears on the card.
- Tapping: shows a 3-screen paywall. Tone is low-pressure: "This is part of Eva's deeper path."
- No pop-ups, no countdowns, no "limited time" pressure.

### Free tier (always available)
- Unlimited daily check-ins.
- Basic 7-day insight view.
- 3 educational articles per month.
- One body awareness exercise per week.

- Advanced pattern analysis: mood × libido × pain × cycle.
- Full educational library access:
  - Self-understanding modules.
  - Pain and discomfort education.
  - Psychological and anxiety content.

  - Relationship and communication path.

- Never block insight screen.
- Premium is additive depth, not a gate on core function.

## 12. UX Metrics Logic

### Retention
- Daily check-in completion is the primary retention signal.
- The product earns return visits by making data more valuable over time — not by sending aggressive notifications or gamifying with streaks.
- Insight quality improves as data volume grows — creating a genuine compounding return reason.
- Onboarding personalization increases first-week retention: users feel the app "knows" them from day one.

### Daily check-in completion
- Check-in designed to complete in under 3 minutes.
- Skip options reduce abandonment — partial check-ins count.
- Single daily notification, neutral language.
- Check-in card always prominent on home screen.
- Re-entry after missed days is frictionless (no onboarding re-entry required).

### Subscription potential
- Premium is introduced only after the user has generated real insights (5+ check-ins).
- Paywall copy connects to personal data: "See how your desire has moved over 30 days."
- The trial removes financial barrier at the moment of curiosity.
- Export feature appeals to healthcare-aware users as a genuine utility value.

### What Eva does not optimize for
- Time-in-app.
- Notification volume.
- Viral sharing.
- Social features.
- Gamification mechanics.

## 13. Risks and Ethical Safeguards

### Medical risk
**Risk:** Users may use Eva's pattern insights as self-diagnosis.
**Safeguard:** All insight language is probabilistic ("may be connected," "often"). Insight screen always includes: "Eva notices patterns — it doesn't diagnose." Professional support card appears at defined thresholds, not on demand.

### Pain escalation risk
**Risk:** User tracks severe or recurring pain without seeking help.
**Safeguard:** Pain logged at 3/5 or higher generates a soft in-app note after first occurrence. If pain is logged 3+ times in 3 weeks: professional support card appears on insight screen. Language is calm, never alarming.

**Threshold triggers for professional support prompt:**
- Recurring pain (3+ occurrences in 3 weeks).
- Libido logged at 0 for 10+ consecutive days with mood logged as "rough" or "low."
- User notes contain keywords related to bleeding, burning, or unusual symptoms (keyword-matched, not AI-analyzed in MVP).
- User selected "there's some history that makes this complicated" in onboarding.

### Trauma risk
**Risk:** Check-in or AI questions could trigger users with trauma history.
**Safeguard:** AI assistant is instructed never to probe for details, never to ask "what happened," and to recommend professional support clearly when trauma-adjacent content appears. Onboarding captures history flag and adjusts tone. No trauma disclosure is ever required.

### Privacy risk
**Risk:** App data could be exposed (device access, iCloud backup, data breach).
**Safeguard:** Biometric/PIN lock at app level. Clear privacy policy. Option to disable iCloud backup of Eva data. Data stored locally with encryption. No external sharing by default.

### Shame amplification risk
**Risk:** Tracking could make users feel worse about themselves (low libido logged repeatedly).
**Safeguard:** Normalization copy is woven into the UI. Low desire does not trigger concern cards unless combined with persistent distress signals. Insight framing is always "this is information," never "this is a problem."

### Coercion risk
**Risk:** Partner could pressure user to share data or use Eva "together."
**Safeguard:** No partner-sharing feature in MVP. Partner context is always optional and logged privately. The app is single-user by design.
